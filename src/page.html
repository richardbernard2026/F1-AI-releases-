Do F1 teams get faster when their AI gets smarter?

No. Across 88 race weekends, a team's results after its AI partner ships a new model look just like its results after any random date. Same story when the models got worse.

By Richard Bernard. Data through the 2026 United States Grand Prix.

06

Why there's no link

The model isn't on the car

Teams use AI for analysis, simulation set-up, software and paperwork. A release on Tuesday doesn't change the parts on the car on Sunday. Those were designed, made and signed off weeks earlier.

The deals are new

Five of the six engineering deals started in 2025 or 2026. Most teams have seen a handful of releases since, which is too few to see anything but a very large effect.

Bigger forces win

Upgrade packages, the 2026 rules reset, the track, weather, crashes and driver form move results far more than a software tool could in a week.

Small samples fool you

An early version of this test flagged Williams as worse after Claude releases. One window straddled the winter break and compared the 2025 car with the 2026 car. Kept inside one season, it vanished. Every window here now stays inside one season.
